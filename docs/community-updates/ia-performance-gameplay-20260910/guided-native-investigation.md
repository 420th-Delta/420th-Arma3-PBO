The IR caller-source case's six HandleDamage callbacks came from a vehicle collision: their ammunition was empty and instigator null. They establish no guided ordnance damage. The earlier IR designation was an empty civilian vehicle. Production `_fn_aim` requires an occupied enemy vehicle known to the caller, so that setup cannot validate actual eligible IR targeting. The focused guided probe uses disabled EAST drivers, heated vehicles and caller reveal, and asserts this designation eligibility.

The first frozen `tests/ia-update/guided-server.sqf` compared both actual guided profiles across five controlled modes: unchanged native parent at 500 m, native parent at 700 m, immediate native-child targeting, native-child targeting after 0.1 seconds, and direct creation of the configured native child. At that stage, the latter three were diagnostic experiments. The test records effective native ammunition configuration, targeting return values, child inheritance, positions/velocities, disappearance, explosions, real ordnance damage and actual production modifier results. Observation is 30 seconds after setup, with bounded trajectory logging and cleanup of created objects. The original failed damage coverage remains a requirement; success of an experimental variant alone does not verify the production worker.

Bohemia documents that [setMissileTarget](https://community.bistudio.com/wiki/setMissileTarget) only works for supported missile types and valid targets within the configured cone; its example includes a delay after creation. The [weapon configuration guidelines](https://community.bistudio.com/wiki/Arma_3%3A_Weapon_Config_Guidelines#Ammo_changes_on_fly_and_on_hit) distinguish a submunition carrier's simulation from the child and describe inherited direction and velocity configuration. These references explain the need to inspect both entities; they do not establish the cause of the missing impacts. No production guidance change was made from that initial evidence alone.
